## Concrete symbolic simplification 1: algebraic wall chains

The checked helper already removes the need for trigonometric angle estimates.
Use the signed vertical components `s_i` of the near-horizontal face frames.
Suppose n squares of common side a > 1 lie in the container, have disjoint
interiors, centers `(x_i,y_i)`, and normal components satisfying

    c_i >= 0, |s_i| <= 1/10, c_i^2 + s_i^2 = 1.

Let all y_i lie in one interval of width h <= 43/100. For adjacent centers,
put X_i = x_(i+1)-x_i >= 0 and Y_i = y_(i+1)-y_i. Suppose bounds L_i <= X_i
<= U_i certify

    (99/100) L_i - (1/10) h > 0,
    (1/10) U_i + h < 1.

The separating-axis theorem for the **original squares** implies that a
forward near-horizontal face normal belonging to one of the two squares
separates this pair. Indeed, both candidate horizontal projections are
positive by the first inequality. Each perpendicular projection has absolute
value below one by the second, whereas a perpendicular support gap is at
least a > 1. This latter fact follows from relative unit normals:
`|dot|+|cross| >= 1`. Thus perpendicular and backward separators are excluded.

Write b_i for the selected normal's vertical component; it is one of s_i or
s_(i+1). The checked `near_axis_pair_gain` supplies

    dot + |cross| >= 1 + (43/50) |s_(i+1)-s_i|.

The exact support gap is a/2 times `1+dot+|cross|`. Since the selected
horizontal component is at most one and X_i is nonnegative,

    X_i >= a + (43/100) a |s_(i+1)-s_i| - b_i Y_i.       (P)

The checked right-wall helper supplies

    4-x_(n-1) >= a/2 + (43/100) a |s_(n-1)|.             (W)

Set V = sum |s_(i+1)-s_i| and T = sum b_i(y_(i+1)-y_i).
Summation by parts after subtracting the first y coordinate gives

    T <= h (|b_(n-2)| + sum |b_i-b_(i-1)|)
      <= h (|s_(n-1)| + V).                            (T)

The second bound is total variation along a nondecreasing subsequence:
the source index of b_i is either i or i+1, so selected source indices never
decrease. Appending the final s_(n-1) and then zero proves the bound by triangle
inequalities. No sine Lipschitz theorem, real angle, or transcendental estimate
is required.

Adding (P) and (W), then applying (T), yields

    4-x_0 >= (n-1/2)a + ((43/100)a-h)(|s_(n-1)|+V)
           > n-1/2.

Hence **x_0 < 4-(n-1/2)**. Reflection gives the dual lower bound on the last
center. All contacts remain allowed; strictness comes solely from a > 1.

This derivation is mathematically complete given the stated SAT bridge and
numeric enclosure premises, but those premises and its Lean implementation
are still work. For the documented chain owners 0,9,2, the research route
reports angle bound 3/32 and common transverse width 0.42900855. Those imply
`|s| < 1/10` and `h < 43/100`, so the rational algebraic theorem is sufficient
for its desired x_0 < 3/2 cut. The reported bounds must still be certified
from actual-packing coverage; they are not asserted proved here.

This is a useful shared theorem for C696/C976/C981 too, provided their own
near-axis and common-range premises can be established. It must not be
assumed that every chain in their research routes meets these constants.

## Concrete symbolic simplification 2: shared ancestors, not duplicated traces

Plan contexts 15, 20, and 21 have exactly the same actual initial state:
resolution 32, sign branch 0, and all 57 ownership vectors. Context 21 repeats
all 758 operations of context 15; context 20 repeats its first 448 operations.
This is confirmed by exact operation-list comparison, not similarity of output
polygons. One theorem for that shared ancestor permits reuse after adding the
explicit ownership restriction. It removes **1,206 duplicate selectors**;
the remaining route contains 4,413 selectors before other minimization.

The semantic lemma is simply: if a sound deduction holds for every packing
in a context, it still holds after restricting that context's auxiliary-vector
set. Reusing a saved numeric state without proof of its ancestry would not
be sound. Other superficially similar traces begin from different vector
projections and cannot be identified without another justification.

This controls repeated proofs as well as data: represent the common-state
derivation once, and inherit its proven enclosure/strict-point results at the
two restricted children. Do not embed thousands of copied polygon states.

## Concrete symbolic simplification 3: pose-independent strict-point facts

The checked `border_center_disk_inside` proves that every closed radius-half
disk about a true square center is strictly inside it because a > 1.
Consequently distinct square centers satisfy squared distance > 1. This
single polynomial theorem works for every orientation and all distance-one
boundary cases. Where the existing border trace ends in a center-distance
contradiction, use it directly with an exact quadratic inequality, rather than
replaying a final angle/pose enumeration.

The two checked missing-face rules also eliminate three of four possible
outside faces from two strict compulsory points. Their zero-angle endpoint
is separately proved and cannot be silently discarded. Reuse this symbolic
face restriction before angle splitting and before any pair checker.

The packaged exact-rational experiment found no initial compulsory-hull
intersection or missing-point contradiction in any of the 57 vectors, and no
usable new four/five/six-point obstruction. Therefore an assertion that the
whole case closes by initial convex-hull membership would contradict current
evidence. Some genuine propagation/strip or stronger analytic lemma remains.

## Size assessment and acceptance boundary

The accepted C977-specific reduction plus new standalone-checked helpers is
**2,191 lines**. A 236 KB operation plan is not intrinsically a multi-million-
line Lean obligation. Four thousand shared route selectors could plausibly be
represented by under 30,000 readable Lean data lines plus shared geometry and
checkers, without hiding giant literals on single lines. That is a design
budget, not a demonstrated complete proof or runtime guarantee.

The bottleneck is missing soundness plus numeric certificate expansion, not
the 912-element coverage table. A compact but computationally enormous
interpreter would not meet the user's compile-efficiency requirement. Before
accepting this route, prove generic preservation lemmas and benchmark one
complete short context with bounded certificates. Count expanded certificate
bytes/nodes and actual kernel time as well as Lean source lines. No honest
whole-proof sub-million-line guarantee follows from this ZIP alone.
