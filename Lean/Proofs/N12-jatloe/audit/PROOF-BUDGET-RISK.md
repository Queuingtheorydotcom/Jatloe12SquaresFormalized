# Independent mathematical assessment of the proof-size budget

This assessment reads the exact counts in `COMPLETE-PROOF-SIZE-BUDGET.md`,
`LEGACY_COMPRESSION.md`, the new shared analytic lemmas, and the small
shortcut archive. It does not run a geometric search or extrapolate a leaf
check to a global proof.

## What is constructively bounded now

The authenticated legacy union has 8,732,801 source lines. That is a real
finite fallback source bound, but it violates the requested efficiency
budget. The compact C898 initial obligation has actually been translated
to124,996 lines, preserving all704 roots. This is a constructive source
cost for that obligation, not an inherent mathematical lower bound.

The C898 pair records currently on disk occupy26,580 lines across14 record
modules (7,759,760 bytes); the13 step wrappers add696 lines. Its new
relaxation family is20,307 lines in35 modules. These were direct text counts
of the assembled sources, not a prediction. They still need semantic and
whole-coverage checking. Their existence supports the feasibility of an
approximately200k-line *single* whole-case translation; it does not bound
the other six historically accepted cases.

With C898's initial obligation alone retained, a300k-line assembly leaves
175,004 lines for every other accepted proof, shared foundations, all later
C898 transitions, and the117-to18 reduction. That arithmetic already makes
uniform mechanical translation a high-risk route to300k. No measured data
currently establishes that it fits.

The complete sub-million requirement has a different and larger domain:
all117 original cases, including the eleven unfinished final obligations.
One must not count only the seven accepted exclusions or only the99-case
baseline reduction. Nor are C981's117 ownership representatives the same
objects as the original117 marker-family cases.

## Which symbolic replacements actually remove whole families

1. **The new four-square contradiction** removes all angle and pair
   certificates for each genuinely matched original case. The archive lists
   twelve expensive shortcut matches. It also explicitly reports matches
   for78,577,585, three of the other40 baseline cases, although their original
   center proofs were already small. Thus15 matches are reported, not15
   newly checked packing adapters. The source of this extra applicability is
   `s12-earlier-case-shortcuts/PROOFS.md`, section “A reusable four-square
   lemma”. This is the strongest demonstrated whole-family saving.
2. **Capacity on one/two cuts** gives a small common terminal for59 cases,
   but only the twelve four-square cases currently bypass the expensive
   enclosure prefix. The remaining47 retain1,914 recorded pair-operation
   selectors, with all-angle coverage and exact intermediate transition
   certificates still missing. Capacity alone is not a bound on that work.
3. **The three-square forced-point theorem** removes substantial terminal
   geometry for the documented C696/C974/C977 representatives. Its signed
   angle, center/radius, follower-section and ancestry premises still need
   proving for the original cases. It does not replace the seven accepted
   unsplit cases or the40 other baseline cases.
4. **Wall forced points plus strict closed-hull transfer** can remove
   geometric prefix steps rather than merely shorten terminal code.
   C898 now has a new original-point reduction occupying every grid slot at
   y=2.5. However, its fourth-long-section implication remains unproved.
   Count this as a precise new target, not a completed case saving.
5. **The C981 midpoint argument** has one complete representative match,
   V116. The previously exhaustive point/segment matching does not support
   promoting it to a shared exclusion of the other representatives.

No newly proved analytic theorem currently eliminates the whole expensive
seven-case legacy family. The shortcut archive's own broad screen did not
close any of those seven with its one-pass capacity method.

## Why small witness dimension does not certify a small whole proof

The support-basis method proves and checks one implication using a constant
number of row indices. This gives a genuine bound on the *size per local
witness*, and eliminates huge rational multipliers. It does not bound the
number of distinct source/neighbor polygons, angle intervals, cuts, branch
nodes, or ownership states. The four-index reconstruction succeeded on all
25,843 C898 pair certificates, an excellent exact result for that finite
set. It does not imply that all other cases have25,843 certificates or fewer.

Similarly, a bound of1,914 recipe selectors does not bound the support
records: one pair operation may quantify over every source pose times
every remaining neighbour pose and every signed axis, with context-specific
cuts and relaxations. State JSON size counts final polygons and selectors,
not all those universal witnesses. Collapsing numeric text or moving it to
long lines does not reduce the corresponding checker work.

The missing historical numerical bytes are97.99% of the intended numeric
input inventory. Source authenticity and old successful receipts establish
historical trust, but neither supplies a record count for a new serializer.
A rigorous sub-million proof-size bound is therefore unavailable now.

## A specific limitation on applying the four-square theorem to C981

Several C981 vectors visually match the horizontally reflected A/B/L/R
ownership pattern. One required excluded point then becomes(3.24,1.87).
C981's corresponding bottom-right corner is a **two-marker** owner, mask12,
not the singleton owner that forces this point in the baseline template.
The axis square `[2.9,3.901] × [0,1.001]` has the correct side and box,
contains the two corner markers `(3,.914),(3.086,1)`, and does not contain
(3.24,1.87). Thus the needed missing-point bridge is not a consequence of
those corner data. It cannot be added silently to get a blanket C981 saving.

## A testable acceptance criterion

For every original case ID, require one of:

- a checked shared analytic theorem with a checked exact packing adapter;
- a complete compact finite certificate DAG, with its actual serialized
  record totals and a complete original-entry replay.

Count the shared union of rows, supports, contexts, cuts, transitions and
fallback terms once, using the real bounded-record serializer. Set explicit
source-byte, term/record and validation-cost budgets as well as line limits.
Only that complete union establishes a constructive upper bound.

The useful next mathematical targets are families with repeated *original
premises*, not families sharing a filename prefix. A good target removes a
whole enclosure chain, as the four-square proof does. If a candidate needs
unchecked tightened boxes or already-pruned long sections, it is merely a
conditional terminal and cannot be credited with that saving.

The evidence supports substantial reductions and a plausible research
route. It does not support a numerical ETA or a promised final line bound.
The unresolved risk is the number of genuinely necessary geometric states,
not the amount of generic Lean boilerplate.
